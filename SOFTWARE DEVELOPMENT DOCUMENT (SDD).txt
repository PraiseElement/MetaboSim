SOFTWARE DEVELOPMENT DOCUMENT (SDD)
Project Title:

MetaboSim: An Interactive Metabolic Pathway Visualizer and Simulator for Biochemistry Education

1. INTRODUCTION
1.1 Background

Metabolism is arguably one of the most intellectually demanding domains in biochemistry. Students frequently resort to rote memorization. They memorize glycolysis. They memorize the TCA cycle. They memorize pathways without understanding regulation, integration, or physiological relevance. The result is fragile knowledge. It breaks under examination pressure.

In many institutions, particularly in resource-limited environments, teaching methods remain static. Diagrams are drawn on boards. Slides are shown. Yet metabolism is dynamic. It is temporal. It responds to environmental and hormonal cues. A static representation cannot convey flux. It cannot convey adaptation.

MetaboSim will address this educational gap by providing a dynamic and interactive metabolic simulation environment. It will allow users to explore, manipulate, and visualize metabolic responses under various physiological and pathological conditions.

1.2 Problem Statement

There is currently a lack of accessible, interactive tools that:

Demonstrate metabolic regulation dynamically.

Integrate multiple pathways into a unified system.

Provide physiological context.

Support African and global curricula.

Enable experiential learning.

Students therefore struggle with:

Conceptual understanding.

Clinical integration.

Retention.

Application in research and medicine.

1.3 Objectives

The primary objective is to develop a web-based educational platform that:

Simulates metabolic pathway dynamics.

Demonstrates regulatory mechanisms.

Allows parameter manipulation.

Connects biochemical processes to real-life physiology and disease.

Secondary objectives include:

Enhancing critical thinking.

Supporting medical and life science students.

Enabling self-paced learning.

Reducing cognitive overload.

1.4 Scope

The system will initially focus on:

Glycolysis.

Gluconeogenesis.

Oxidative Phosphorylation.

Future expansion may include:

Lipid metabolism.

Amino acid metabolism.

Hormonal integration.

Cancer metabolism.
